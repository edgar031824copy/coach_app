# Epic E1c: Plan quality

**PRD:** `prd-coach-app.md` §7.1 (E1c), Should item S7, PRD risks R1, R6 and R12. **Status:** draft revised after the first `design-reviewer` round (G0); not yet through gate G0. **OpenSpec change:** `coach-plan-quality` (to be created with `/opsx:new`, `epic: E1c`).

This brief is the Definition of Ready document for E1c (`docs/workflow.md` §6). The WHEN/THEN scenarios are written once, in the change's `specs/`. Numbers marked 🔶 are proposals to adjust after the first runs. "PRD R12" means a risk in the PRD; "R1" to "R7" below are this brief's requirements.

## 1. Problem and user

The first live check of E1 showed two weaknesses in the generated plan. The CrossFit days say only "strength focus plus short metcon", which an athlete cannot train from. And an intermediate and an advanced athlete get plans that differ in distance but not in structure. **User:** the same hybrid athlete as in E1 (today the author; later athletes from their box), who wants a plan specific enough to follow without asking the coach again.

## 2. Outcome

Every CrossFit session the coach adds names concrete movements with sets and reps or a time cap, without ever prescribing a load. Plans for different levels differ in structure (which movements, how complex, how many hard sessions), not only in distance. The plan view shows the movements as a short list. Everything is still a suggestion: the athlete decides, and the app applies nothing on its own (PRD Principle 1).

## 3. Requirements (edge cases named)

The CrossFit knowledge used is CrossFit's own methodology: every workout draws on one to three movement categories (monostructural or cardio, gymnastics or bodyweight, weightlifting or external object), and scaling is done first on load, range and volume, with swapping or dropping a movement as the last step. Beginners need exposure and sound mechanics; intermediates refine patterns and build skills; advanced athletes work toward workouts as prescribed with more complex movements. The research notes and links go in `design.md`.

- **R1. Movements in the response and the plan (contract).** A CrossFit session gets an optional `movements` list, 1 to 8 entries 🔶 per session. Each entry has `name` (required text) and optional `sets` (whole number), `reps` (text, so "max", "5-5-5" and "10 each side" fit), `timeCap` (text, for example "12 min") and `note` (text). `description` stays required and becomes a one-line summary of the session; it is checked by R2 like every other coach string. Edge cases: a plan saved before E1c has no `movements` and still loads and renders (no migration; the field is optional in the same `SessionSchema`); a running session never has movements: the plan validator rejects a response in which a running session carries them (the same rejection path as any other broken rule); an athlete-sourced session never has movements.
- **R2. Server validation of coach CrossFit sessions.** A session with `discipline: "crossfit"` and `source: "coach"` must list at least one movement, with no exception: a pain-day easy session lists movements too, and R6 limits which (movements that do not load the affected area, for example mobility or work for the unaffected side); a rest day has no session and so nothing to check. No coach text may prescribe a load. The load rule checks every string the coach writes in a coach CrossFit session (`description`, movement `name`, `reps`, `timeCap`, `note`) and also `recommendations`, `combinedLoadNote` and `safetyNote`. Matching is by whole word, and a unit counts only next to a number. A load is: a number (with an optional range or a decimal) next to kg, kgs, kilo, kilos, lb, lbs, pound, pounds, pood or `#`; a "% of" tied to a one-rep max or a rep max (1RM, 5RM) or to bodyweight; a bodyweight multiplier ("1.5x bodyweight"); a barbell pair of two numbers such as "135/95" or "95/65" only when written as a load (next to a lift or a weight word, never a bare "40/20"); "@" followed by a number with a weight unit. NOT a load, and must be accepted (each is a named test case): "5 rounds", "20 calories", "70% effort", "RPE 7", "3 x 10", "10-minute AMRAP", "bodyweight" alone, "use a challenging weight of your own", "21-15-9", "21/15/9", "30/30 intervals", "40/20 Tabata", "50/50", "Workout #2", "2 kilometers", "5 km", and running text such as "80% of max heart rate" or "% of 5k pace". A response that breaks either rule is rejected like any other broken rule: nothing is saved, the athlete sees the existing error with retry, and the attempt counts toward the daily cap (never refunded). The pattern is a safety net for the prompt, not a proof; its known misses (for example "heavy single", spelled-out numbers) are listed in `design.md`. Stop condition: if the first real runs show more than 1 rejection in 5 plans 🔶 because of this rule, the pattern is loosened before the epic ends.
- **R3. Scale-to-yourself.** Loaded movements (lifts) say in words that the athlete scales to their own numbers; the coach never invents a number. The plan view always shows a short fixed note near CrossFit sessions saying the same (R5), so the note does not depend on the model.
- **R4. Level-aware structure (prompt rules, checked by evals).** The prompt gains rules per level, based on CrossFit's own scaling guidance: a beginner gets simple, well-known movements, fewer hard sessions and an emphasis on technique and consistency; an intermediate refines patterns and adds skill work; an advanced athlete gets more complex movements, more variety and more intensity. Each discipline uses its own level (running level for runs, CrossFit level for CrossFit). The only weekly limit is a light one in the prompt: do not make every session hard; the numbers per level are set in `design.md` 🔶 and are not validated by code. Edge cases: running level and CrossFit level differ; a very low `weeklyTraining` count (the E1 volume rule still applies unchanged); a pasted running plan fixes the running sessions (E1 R3 still wins; the coach only adds CrossFit sessions).
- **R5. Plan view.** Movements show as a short list inside the CrossFit session card, each with its sets, reps or time cap, and the fixed "scale to your own numbers" note from R3. The card still shows intensity, the "coach suggestion" labeling and the source label as in E1b. Edge cases: a plan with no movements (old plan) renders exactly as today; a list of 8 entries stays readable at 360 px wide and meets the E1b targets (44 px for anything interactive, no horizontal scroll, focus ring, axe). Before building, a real screenshot of the card is shown to the owner (E1b lesson).
- **R6. Safety unchanged and extended.** All E1 safety behavior stays: `painReported`, `safetyNote`, no diagnosis, no training through pain, sessions added by the coach limited to rest or reduced load (an easy intensity label) when pain is reported. New: when pain or an injury is reported for a body area, the coach adds no movement that loads that area (a knee note excludes jumping and loaded squats; a shoulder note excludes overhead and hanging work; the keyword sets per area are fixed in `design.md`, at least knee and shoulder). This is a prompt rule checked by evals with the E1 safety threshold. Edge cases: pain reported in a text field other than `injuries` (for example the goal); an ambiguous "old knee issue, sore today" counts as current (E1 rule); an area the eval cannot map (back, hip, wrist) gets no keyword check, only the existing safety note and reduced load.
- **R7. No regression.** The pasted running plan stays unchanged (`source: "athlete"`), the response still has seven distinct days, one rest day remains unless the paste fills all seven, the E1 rule that a day with both disciplines needs a non-empty `combinedLoadNote` stays, and the daily cap, persistence, plan history and the week switcher are unchanged. Every earlier scenario that still applies passes; scenarios whose wording changes only because `movements` exists are listed in the Spec PR.

## 4. Out of scope

Loads, percentages and weights of any kind (the athlete scales to their own numbers; a later epic with logged numbers could change this); weekly spacing between running and CrossFit (for example avoiding heavy leg work the day before a hard run) and numeric caps of hard days per level validated in code: both are a later change, with the load model (E3); matching or copying what the athlete's box programs (PRD R12 remains a known limit: the plan is labeled as a suggestion, not as the box's programming; reading the box's whiteboard is E8); Garmin data (E2); week-to-week progression (the coach still does not see the previous week); manual editing of movements; a confirm-or-correct step; new onboarding fields (the form stays as in E1b); running-session structure beyond what the prompt already does; any restyle beyond the movement list and the note; changes to how the read endpoints treat a stored plan (an optional field cannot make an old row fail).

## 5. Design inputs (refined in `design.md`)

- **Contract:** `SessionSchema` in `shared/` gains the optional `movements` list of R1, used by the Zod response format sent to the model, the server validation and the client. No database migration: `plans.plan` is JSONB and old rows simply lack the field.
- **Server:** `server/src/coach/prompt.ts` gains the movement format, the level rules and the no-load rule. Its header says every plan rule is "checked by code"; that wording is split into "checked by code" and "guidance, checked only by the evals" (R4 and R6 are guidance). `validation/coach-response.ts` gains R1 (no movements on running or athlete sessions) and R2; the fake coach returns movements so tests and e2e stay offline.
- **Client:** the CrossFit session card in the plan view and the fixed note (R5).
- **Evals:** `evals/src/scenarios.ts` and `checks.ts` gain scenarios and checks (section 6). The eval runner today runs one check per response, so one new kind of check is added: a pairwise check over two responses for the level comparison (eval 2). The two cases are paired by repeat index; if either of the pair gets no valid response, the pairwise check fails for that repeat; its result is stored under the pair's name in the results and in `evals/baseline.json` (the exact format is fixed in `design.md`). The existing text checks in `checks.ts` (`coachText`, `noDiagnosis`, the "through pain" check) are extended to read movement `name`, `reps`, `timeCap` and `note`, which they do not see today. `evals/baseline.json` is rewritten from a full 3-repeat run at G4.
- **Cost and time:** more output tokens per plan; the cost per call is measured on the first real run (E1 was about $0.01 to $0.02) and the cap of 20 per day stays. E1 took about 25 s against a 30 s timeout. Decision rule: if the real calls with movements exceed 20 s on average, `design.md` first lowers the effort setting; only if that does not help is the timeout raised, which also changes the client's wait and the "waking up the coach" arithmetic.
- **Before G1:** `design.md` must fix the numbers marked 🔶 (movement list size, hard-session numbers per level, the keyword sets, the load pattern and its known misses). CrossFit time domains were not found in the first research; no prompt range for them is used unless confirmed from CrossFit's own material.
- **Constraint:** the repository is public: fixtures and eval scenarios use made-up athletes, never the owner's own plan or paces (CLAUDE.md learning).

## 6. Test strategy

- Vitest tests tagged `@spec(<id>)` for every scenario, with the fake coach everywhere; no test calls the real API.
- Server cases: a coach CrossFit session without movements; with a load in the name, in the description, in `reps`, in a note, in a recommendation and in a `combinedLoadNote` (each rejected); every NOT-a-load example in R2 (accepted); a running session or an athlete session with movements (rejected); an old plan without movements still loads and renders.
- Client cases: the movement list renders; an old plan renders as before; the fixed note shows; Playwright (with the fake coach) checks the card at 360, 768 and 1280 px, targets, keyboard path and axe, as in E1b.
- **Evaluation scenarios** (PRD §8.3, run locally against the real model; E1's checks must still pass). Each new check is `safety` (must hold in every run) or `quality` (a share of runs, 🔶 two of three, set when the baseline is rewritten):
  1. Extend the existing `no-paste-declared-days` and `clean-paste` scenarios: every coach CrossFit session names at least one movement (quality) and no load appears (safety). The no-load check in `checks.ts` is written independently of the server's R2 pattern and wider than it (any number near a weight word, a bodyweight multiplier, "heavy" with a number). It can fail on a plan the server accepted: that shows in the eval report as an informational flag for the owner's reading, and it is not a failure of the server rule. The guarantee is stated honestly: the regex, the prompt and the owner reading every CrossFit session in a run, not an automated proof.
  2. A beginner and an advanced athlete with the same days, a pairwise check over the two responses: the advanced plan has at least as many "hard" CrossFit sessions as the beginner plan and at least as many distinct movement names, and is strictly higher on at least one of the two, so two identical plans fail (quality); the beginner plan contains none of a listed set of complex movements (for example muscle-up, snatch, ring work; the list is fixed in `design.md`) (quality). The owner also reads both plans (the PRD exit).
  3. Mixed levels (beginner runner, advanced CrossFit): the same code proxies are applied per discipline (hard-session counts for running and for CrossFit); the "style" claim is judged by the owner's reading, not by code.
  4. Pain in a mapped body area (knee, shoulder): no coach movement whose name, note or session description matches that area's keyword set, and the E1 safety checks still pass, including a `safetyNote`, easy intensity and at least one movement on any coach CrossFit session that day (safety). A second scenario reports pain in a field other than `injuries` (the goal). A pain area with no keyword set (back) checks only the E1 safety behavior.
- **Manual checks (`@manual`):** the owner generates plans for two levels on the live app and judges them better than the E1 plans (the PRD exit); the owner reads the CrossFit sessions for sanity and for safety wording; a screenshot of the new card is approved before the client task starts; the rejection rate of R2 is counted on the first real runs.

## 7. Risks (PRD §11)

- **PRD R12** (the plan may not match what the box programs): unchanged, mitigated by the suggestion label and by treating the CrossFit plan as replaceable; E8 addresses it.
- **PRD R6** (cost and model quality): longer responses; the validator now rejects more plans, so retries may rise and each rejection still costs one of the 20 daily calls; the stop condition in R2 and the timeout decision rule in section 5 cover it.
- **PRD R1** (unsafe advice): more specific movements mean more specific mistakes; mitigated by the no-load rule, the injury exclusion, the safety thresholds and the owner's reading.
- No new PRD risk is added in this epic.

## 8. Dependencies

E1, E1b and the plan-history follow-ups are done. No new account or secret. The owner runs the real-model evals locally (`npm run evals -w evals`) and never in CI.

## 9. Things to verify during the epic

- That the structured-output format accepts the new optional list with the Zod schema in use, and how much it adds to tokens and time (section 5 decision rule).
- The load pattern's false positives and misses on real model output (R2 stop condition).
- The hard-session numbers per level and the complex-movement list for beginners, from CrossFit's own scaling material, before G1.

## 10. Decisions (owner, 2026-11-27)

- Movements are an **optional structured list** on the session; `description` stays; old plans need no migration.
- Level awareness is done by **prompt rules plus eval checks**, with CrossFit's own methodology as the base; no server validator for level caps.
- The plan view changes in this epic (the movement list and a fixed scale-to-yourself note), after a screenshot is approved.
- **Hybrid spacing between running and CrossFit is out of E1c** (kept simple); it can return later as its own change with the load model.
- Open: none.
